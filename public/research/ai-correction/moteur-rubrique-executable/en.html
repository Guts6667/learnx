Why LearnX no longer asks the model to grade.

False passes, false fails and double penalties share a cause: the model interpreted the rubric freely. We decided to remove that authority from models.

Decision from 14 August 2026Retrospectively published 21 August 2026Version 1

The model judged too many things at once

In early protocols, one model selected levels, explained them, proposed quotations, estimated confidence and decided on a second pass. Even with a strict schema, one weakness could contaminate several criteria and an unwritten requirement could appear in feedback.

The target is no longer “find the best judge”, but reduce what the model is allowed to decide.

The rubric becomes executable

Each criterion is decomposed into atomic elements with a stable key, owner, evidence rules, acceptable variants, contradictions and authored remediation. The compiler checks that every level is reachable, adding evidence cannot lower a result, and one gap cannot be penalised twice.

The model finds or challenges evidence. LearnX validates evidence, applies the level table and renders template-based feedback. No model directly produces a final level, score, pass/fail, progression effect or published free text.

Abstention becomes a product function

An element can be supported, explicitly refuted, not demonstrated or ambiguous. LearnX computes every possible resolution of ambiguity. If all resolutions yield the same level, feedback can be published. If the level changes, no exact score is shown and a targeted clarification is requested.

Not every activity is eligible

  • Fully compilable: levels and indicative score can be calculated.
  • Partially compilable: local feedback without a global score.
  • Unsupported autonomously: no public AI correction.

Presence of a choice, target, indicator or justification are good candidates. Originality, reflective depth and holistic professional relevance must not be forced into mechanical contracts.

What LearnX promises — and does not promise

The engine can assess compliance with a sufficiently formalised rubric. It does not prove full mastery when the rubric covers only part of a competence. Feedback remains strictly formative and never writes progression.

Selected promise

“Autonomous, criterion-based formative feedback grounded in evidence found in the learner’s response.”
