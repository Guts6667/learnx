Are two models better than one?

We simulated a pipeline where Mistral grades and Sonnet verifies sensitive cases. The result is more cautious, but not reliable enough to become a product.

The hypothesis

Mistral Medium 3.5 was fast, technically reliable and inexpensive, but passed some weak responses. Sonnet 4.6 was more conservative and had no observed false pass, but rejected more acceptable responses. Could a targeted cascade combine their strengths?

A conservative verifier can detect risk. That does not automatically make it a reliable arbitrator.

What the historical recombination shows

Across 72 existing cells, Mistral alone produced 3 false passes and 5 false fails. Sonnet alone produced 0 false passes and 6 false fails, with one finally unusable output. The targeted cascade removed observed false passes and reduced certain false fails to 3, but classified 8 disagreements as uncertain.

Always choosing the stricter opinion would increase unjustified rejections. Always choosing Sonnet would merely add Mistral’s cost without useful independence.

Cost does not necessarily double

In the targeted simulation, Sonnet was called on only some cases. Average cost including prudent loading and FX buffer was about USD 0.0161 per correction, versus 0.0077 for Mistral alone and 0.0239 for Sonnet alone. Yet cascade P90 could exceed historical Sonnet: a lower average does not automatically lower the reservation ceiling.

Why this is not production evidence

  • Recombined campaigns did not use exactly the same prompt or protocol identity.
  • Models shared some severity and double-penalty biases.
  • Two diverging opinions provide neither majority nor reliable arbitrator.
  • Eight abstentions could harm the experience if frequent in production.

Decision

The Mistral–Sonnet composite was not promoted. Independent verification remains valuable, but only if models no longer decide the final level themselves. This led us toward a deterministic rubric engine.

Status

Indicative simulation, never reclassified as a comparable benchmark or pricing basis.
