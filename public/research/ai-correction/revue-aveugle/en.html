What blind review changed in our protocol.

Before comparing more models, we had to verify that our expectations did not force a single pedagogical interpretation and that review packages did not expose the gold labels.

The benchmark was part of the experiment

An initial review showed that some model disagreements came from debatable gold boundaries. More importantly, the four responses labelled “ambiguous” could be graded confidently: learner hesitation did not mean the evaluator needed a second pass.

Response ambiguity and evaluation uncertainty are different phenomena.

Three methodological defects

An artificial second pass

The corpus expected a second pass for all four ambiguous cases even though the rubrics enabled stable classification. The gate measured a corpus convention rather than evaluator quality.

Criteria contaminating each other

A missing target could lower both the indicator and plan coherence. A concise reflection could be penalised for not specifying a future action owned by another criterion.

A falsely blind package

The first review artefact still contained expectations and rationales. That review was invalidated, and the generator was corrected before a genuinely blind reading.

What changed

  • Second pass was separated from the “ambiguous” category and derived from explicit signals.
  • Each criterion received a penalty owner to prevent double penalties.
  • Blind packages exclude model, provider, gold, category and cost.
  • Boundary disagreements are separated from serious failures.
  • A distinct holdout was prepared to avoid promoting on tuning cases.

What this review is not

These research audits do not create a human correction service. LearnX targets an autonomous formative system that can abstain. Synthetic corpora and pseudo-oracles test contractual compliance; they do not prove universal pedagogical truth.

Decision

Never lower a threshold to pass a model; first correct the protocol when the target itself is inconsistent.
