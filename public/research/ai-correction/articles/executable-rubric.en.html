Architecture change

From LLM judge to executable rubric engine.

We abandon the requirement to score every free-form response. Models find and challenge evidence; LearnX applies deterministic rules.

20 August 2026Evidence-assist 3.0Strictly formative feedback

New authority split

  • A versioned rubric defines atomic elements, examples, counterexamples and rules.
  • A researcher proposes exact spans from the response.
  • An independent falsifier looks for contradictions or insufficient evidence.
  • LearnX validates spans and computes levels through rules.
  • Feedback comes from authored templates and never changes progress.

A revealing first test

Two of four calls ran before stopping: the positive case found 9 elements out of 9; the negative case 7 out of 9. Part of the disagreement came from an oracle that did not distinguish absence from explicit refutation.

Semantic arbitrationEXPLICITLY_REFUTED becomes distinct from NOT_DEMONSTRATED, while provisionally allowing the same pedagogical effect. The nuance remains in the certificate and feedback.

The promise this supports

“Autonomous, criteria-based formative feedback grounded in evidence from the learner’s response.” Never certified correction or AI validation of mastery.
